# EV Infrastructure Planning Guidelines

## Introduction
This document establishes standard industry guidelines for Electric Vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure planning.

## High-Demand Mitigation
When projected demand exceeds historical or threshold averages, prompt expansion of infrastructure is critical to prevent queuing and ensure customer satisfaction.

1. **Threshold Rule**: If average demand surpasses 35 kWh during peak periods, the location requires immediate expansion.
2. **Expansion Protocol**: Priority should be given to Level 3 Direct Current Fast Chargers (DCFC). Adding 2 to 4 DCFC units can mitigate up to 40% of queuing delays.
3. **Resource Siting**: New chargers should be sited on pre-existing heavy load grid nodes to avoid costly substation upgrades.

## Weekend Load Balancing
Weekend charging behaviors typically exhibit different usage peaks than weekday commuter charging.
1. **Dynamic Pricing Integration**: Implement off-peak pricing drops to incentivize shifting charging from midday weekend peaks to early morning or late night.
2. **Battery Energy Storage System (BESS)**: For locations where weekend peak demand consistently exceeds grid capacity, deploy localized BESS to shave peaks. BESS can charge overnight on Friday and discharge during the Saturday/Sunday midday peaks.

## Scheduling and Operations Optimization
- **Smart Queueing**: Locations exceeding average demand should deploy app-based reservation systems. Assign strict 45-minute limits per session during high-load periods.
- **Maintenance Prioritization**: High-load stations must have a Service Level Agreement (SLA) of 99% uptime, necessitating proactive maintenance checks every 14 days rather than the standard 30-day cycle.
